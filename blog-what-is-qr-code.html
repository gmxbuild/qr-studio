What Is a QR Code and How Does It Work?

Updated January 2025 · 5 min read

You have probably seen them everywhere — on restaurant menus, product packaging, business cards, posters, and even TV ads. Those small black-and-white square patterns are called QR codes. But what exactly are they, and how do they turn a simple camera scan into a website, Wi-Fi connection, or contact card?

In this guide, we will explain QR codes in plain English, cover their history, and show you how they work behind the scenes.

What Does QR Stand For?

QR stands for Quick Response. The name reflects the original goal of the technology: to store information that can be scanned and read faster than a traditional barcode.

A Brief History

QR codes were invented in 1994 by Masahiro Hara, an engineer at the Japanese company Denso Wave. At that time, the company was looking for a way to track car parts during manufacturing. Traditional barcodes were too limited — they could only store a small amount of numbers.

Hara designed a two-dimensional code that could store much more information (including text and Kanji characters) and be scanned from any angle. The company chose not to enforce its patent, which allowed QR codes to spread freely across the world. Today, they are a global standard.

How Does a QR Code Store Information?

A QR code is a grid of small black and white squares called modules. Each module represents a binary value — black for 1, white for 0. Together, these modules encode the actual data.

Three large squares are placed in the top-left, top-right, and bottom-left corners. These are called finder patterns, and they help the scanner detect the code and understand its orientation, no matter how the phone is held.

QR codes also include error correction data. This means that even if up to 30% of the code is damaged, dirty, or covered, it can still be scanned successfully. This is why logos can be placed in the center of many modern QR codes.

How Does Scanning Work?

When you open your phone's camera and point it at a QR code, the following happens:

  1. The camera captures the image of the code.
  2. The software detects the finder patterns to locate the code.
  3. It reads the grid of black and white modules and translates them into binary data.
  4. The binary data is converted into readable content — a URL, text, phone number, or other data.
  5. The phone acts on that content — opening a browser, dialer, or app.

The entire process takes less than a second on a modern phone.

What Can a QR Code Contain?

QR codes can store many different types of information:

  • Website URLs — the most common use today
  • Plain text — messages, notes, codes
  • Wi-Fi credentials — network name and password for one-tap connection
  • Contact details — name, phone, email as a vCard
  • Phone numbers — for quick dialing
  • SMS or email messages — with pre-filled text
  • Geographic coordinates — opening a location in Maps
  • Payment information — used heavily in Asia

Why Are QR Codes So Popular Now?

QR codes were invented in 1994, but they only became mainstream around 2020. There are three main reasons:

  • Smartphone cameras improved. Modern phones can scan QR codes natively without any separate app.
  • The pandemic accelerated adoption. Restaurants needed contactless menus, businesses needed touch-free payments.
  • Marketing evolved. Brands realized QR codes bridge the physical and digital worlds effortlessly.

Static vs Dynamic QR Codes

There are two types of QR codes you will encounter:

Static QR codes have the data encoded directly into the pattern. They never expire and do not require any server to function. The QR Studio generator creates static codes.

Dynamic QR codes point to a short URL that redirects to the final destination. This lets you change the destination later, but it requires a paid subscription and stops working if the service shuts down.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do QR codes expire?

Static QR codes never expire. They continue to work as long as the destination they point to is available. Dynamic QR codes can expire if the subscription ends.

Are QR codes safe to scan?

QR codes themselves are just containers of data. The safety depends on what they contain. Always preview the URL before opening it, and avoid scanning codes from unknown or untrusted sources.

How much data can a QR code hold?

A standard QR code can store up to 4,296 alphanumeric characters, 7,089 numeric characters, or about 3 KB of binary data. In practice, shorter content scans faster and more reliably.

Conclusion

QR codes are a simple but brilliant technology. They compress a large amount of information into a small, scannable pattern that works offline, on any phone, from any angle. Whether you are running a business, sharing a Wi-Fi password, or promoting an event, QR codes remain one of the fastest ways to connect the physical world with the digital one.
